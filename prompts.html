Activity 2 · AI Action Plan
Turn the best opportunity into a 90-day plan with owners, milestones and a stop rule.
Step 1 · Lab 7 · input
Mine Your Business Process from Dormant Data
Look at my emails and calendar from the last 30 days about [process, e.g. 'client quotations']. Reconstruct how this process actually runs: the trigger, each step, who does it, the handoffs, how long things wait, and where work is redone. Show it as a numbered list, then list the three biggest frictions with the evidence (email subject or meeting) for each.
Check: Each step and friction cites a real email or meeting; you corrected anything Muse guessed. 
Step 2 · Lab 8 · input
Draft Your Company Map
For the process [process name], draft a company map for an AI agent: (1) the 6 key entities, (2) a definition of 5 terms the way my company uses them - ask me when unsure instead of guessing, (3) how the entities relate, (4) the approvals and thresholds that apply, (5) the events that should update the map in real time.
Check: Muse asked at least one clarifying question; your corrections are in the final map. 
Step 3 · Lab 9 · input
Draft the 90-Day AI Action Plan
Using this opportunity [paste your top opportunity from Activity 1] and this company map [paste your company map from Lab 8], draft a 90-day action plan: objective with baseline and target, where agents integrate in the process (inform, recommend, prepare, execute with approval, execute with monitoring), digital architecture (model, company map, live events, connectors, guardrails), 30/60/90-day milestones, one DRI with decision rights, risks and controls, KPIs, cost and value, and a kill criterion.
Check: Every milestone has an owner and a measurable result; there is a kill criterion.

Tell every stakeholder what changes for them, then run the company on DRIs, agents and honest numbers.

Check: Three drafts; nothing sent; each message says what changes and what does not. 
Step 2 · Lab 12 · input
Run the Weekly Innovation Review
Every Friday I review our AI initiative [initiative name]. From this week's emails and meetings, prepare: KPIs mentioned vs target, blockers, decisions I must make, and new ideas staff raised (with who raised them). End with one question I should ask the team on Monday.

Check: Your final decision is in your own words; you can name what changed it.

Lab 1 · Topic 1
Join Meta Muse, Your Always-On Agent
Introduce yourself in three sentences: what you can do for a business manager, what you need access to, and what you will always ask me before doing.

Lab 2 · Topic 1
Connect Gmail and Google Calendar
Confirm which of my apps you are connected to, and what you can and cannot do in each one.
Check: Gmail and Calendar show as connected; Muse states it cannot send without your approval. 
Lab 3 · Topic 1
Search and Summarise Your Email
Search my Gmail for emails from the last 7 days about [a client, project or supplier]. Summarise them in five bullet points: who, what they need, by when, and the action for me. Quote the subject line of each email you used.
Check: Every bullet names a real email subject; you found no invented details. 
Lab 4 · Topic 1
Triage Your Inbox Down to What Matters
Triage my inbox down to only what matters. Mute newsletters, promotions and routine alerts. Keep a ranked list of the five emails that need me, with one line on why each matters, and keep it current as new mail arrives. Rule: anything from [your boss's name] is always in the top three.
Check: A ranked list of up to five emails, each with a reason; newsletters are excluded. 
Lab 5 · Topic 1
Your Morning Brief: Calendar Plus Inbox
Give me tomorrow's brief: my meetings in time order with the prep each one needs, any clashes or back-to-back meetings with no travel time, and the emails I must reply to before each meeting. Keep it under 150 words.
Check: The brief matches your real calendar; clashes are flagged, not resolved for you.

Business Use-Case Sprints
Always-on agents across the business. Muse drafts; you decide.
Sprint 1 · Sales
Follow Up Every Lead
Find emails from prospects or customers in the last 14 days that I have not replied to. Rank them by likely deal value and urgency. For the top three, draft a short follow-up that answers their question and proposes a next step. Do not send anything.
Check: Every lead is a real unanswered email; drafts stay drafts. You decide: You choose which follow-ups to send. 
Sprint 2 · Customer service
Calm the Angry Customer
Find complaint or problem emails from the last 30 days. Classify each as urgent, normal or low and say why. Draft an empathetic reply for the two most urgent, and tell me which ones need a phone call from a person instead of an email.
Check: Urgency is justified from the email itself; no promises of refunds or compensation. You decide: You approve every reply and make the calls. 
Sprint 3 · Marketing
A Week of Posts in Your Voice
I am launching [product or service] for [audience] on [date]. Draft five social posts for the week: two for LinkedIn, three for Instagram, each with a hook, one benefit, a call to action and suggested image. Keep my brand voice: [three brand-voice words]. No health, price or competitor claims I have not given you.
Check: Every claim traces to what you supplied; nothing is posted. You decide: You approve, edit or reject each post. 
Sprint 4 · HR
Book the Interviews Without the Email Ping-Pong
I need to interview three candidates next week, 45 minutes each. Find free slots in my calendar between 10:00 and 17:00 that leave 15 minutes between meetings. Propose two slots per candidate and draft the invitation email for each. Do not send invites or create events until I approve.
Check: Slots really are free in your calendar; no events created yet. You decide: You confirm slots and send. 
Sprint 5 · Finance
Chase the Cash
Search my email for invoices, quotations and payment reminders from the last 60 days. Make a table: customer or supplier, amount, due date, paid or not (say 'unknown' if unsure). For anything overdue that we are owed, draft a polite reminder. Do not send.
Check: Amounts and dates match the source emails; 'unknown' where unsure. You decide: Finance confirms before anything goes out. 
Sprint 6 · Procurement and operations
Catch the Supplier Delay Before the Customer Does
Scan my emails with suppliers from the last 30 days for promised delivery dates, changes and delays. List each order with the latest promised date and whether it has slipped. For any slip, suggest who must be told and draft that message. Keep watching and alert me when a new delay arrives.
Check: Each order cites the supplier email; the watch is set or run on demand. You decide: You decide the customer message and any change of supplier. 
Sprint 7 · Meetings
Turn Minutes into Actions
Here are my notes from today's meeting [paste notes or transcript]. Extract the decisions, the action items with owner and deadline, and the open questions. Draft the follow-up email to attendees, and propose calendar reminders for each deadline. Ask me before creating anything.
Check: Every action has an owner and a date; nothing invented. You decide: You confirm owners and send the follow-up. 
Sprint 8 · Strategy
Watch the Competition
Track these three competitors: [three competitor names]. Summarise their latest news, launches and price moves with sources (if you cannot search the web, use these links: [paste competitor links]). End with one opportunity or threat for my business and one action I should consider. Then build it as a Space I can refresh each week.
Check: Every point has a source; opinion is labelled as opinion. You decide: You decide whether to act.
